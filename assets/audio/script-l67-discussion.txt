Narrator: Listen to Zara and Hina discuss an important question. Should schools limit mobile phone use during school hours? Listen carefully to both opinions. After listening, decide who you agree with, and why.
Zara: I think schools should limit mobile phones during school hours. When students keep checking their phones, they stop paying attention in class. I have seen this in our own classroom. During maths, at least five students were secretly texting under their desks, and none of them could answer the teacher's question.
Hina: I understand your point, Zara, but a complete limit is too strict. Phones can help us learn. Last week, our science teacher asked us to photograph leaves in the school garden for our project. We could not have done that without our phones. The problem is not the phone. The problem is how we use it.
Zara: That is true for special activities, Hina. But those are rare. Every day, phones mostly distract us. Also, think about break time. Students sit alone staring at screens instead of talking and playing with friends. School should be a place for real conversations, not silent scrolling.
Hina: You make a fair point about break time. Maybe the answer is not a full ban, but clear rules. For example, phones stay switched off in bags during lessons, but we can use them for class projects when the teacher allows it. That way, we get the benefits without the distraction.
Zara: Hmm. Clear rules could work, but only if everyone follows them. Teachers already have so much to manage. Still, your idea is better than doing nothing. Perhaps we can try your rule for one month and see if our class results improve.
Narrator: You have heard both sides. Now discuss with a partner. Who do you agree with, Zara or Hina? Give one reason for your choice, and one question you would ask the other speaker.
